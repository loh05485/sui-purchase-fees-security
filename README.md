## Buying SUI through spot trading

Spot trading gives you more control over execution than a simple instant-buy form.

You first deposit or hold an eligible balance, then open the SUI spot market available to your account. Depending on the market, you may buy SUI with a stablecoin, fiat currency, or another supported asset.

The two order types most buyers encounter are:

### Market order

A market order attempts to execute immediately against available orders in the order book. It is convenient, but the final price can differ from the number visible when you begin the order, especially during fast price movements or periods of lower liquidity.

Market orders are generally executed as taker orders because they remove liquidity from the order book. OKX explains that taker fees apply when an order is filled immediately against existing orders.

### Limit order

A limit order lets you specify the maximum price you are willing to pay. If the market does not reach that price, the order may remain open or expire without being filled.

A limit order is not automatically a maker order. If it matches immediately with an existing order, it can still be treated as a taker order. If it rests on the order book and is filled later, it may receive maker treatment.

For a first purchase, a market order is easier to understand. A limit order is more useful when you care about entry price and are willing to wait.

## How to avoid common mistakes when buying SUI

### Buying because of a short-term price move

SUI is a volatile digital asset. A lower price does not automatically mean the token is cheap, and a rising chart does not prove that the trend will continue.

Decide in advance how much you are willing to risk. If you are uncertain about timing, splitting a planned purchase into several smaller orders can reduce the pressure to choose one exact entry price. It does not remove market risk.

### Confusing spot SUI with leveraged products

If your goal is to own SUI, use a spot purchase rather than a futures or leveraged position. Futures and derivatives involve different fee calculations, funding costs, liquidation risk, and position mechanics. OKX documents separate fee rules for spot, futures, options, and other instruments.

Leverage can make a small market move produce a much larger gain or loss. It is not necessary for buying and holding the token.

### Ignoring the final received amount

Do not look only at the headline price. Review the final SUI amount after fees, the total amount charged, and any withdrawal cost.

This is especially important with card purchases, where the payment route can include a processing charge that is not visible in a simple spot-price comparison.

### Sending SUI to an incompatible wallet

Only withdraw to a wallet that explicitly supports Sui. Check the network, destination address, and minimum withdrawal amount before confirming.
